| 2026-09-25 | Figures are generated by a script in the repo, in greyscale, and committed | draw them by hand in a graphics tool | A figure that regenerates from code cannot drift from what the pipeline actually does, and greyscale means it survives a monochrome print of the paper |

Own-post similarity stays as a secondary on TPs, the 0.78 comparison |

| 2026-09-25 | Bootstrap resamples posts, not (post, symptom) items | item-level resampling, as implemented | The prereg already said posts. Items from one post are correlated: a synthetic check with 2 correlated items per post gave an item-level interval 27% narrower than the post-level one |
| 2026-09-25 | Erasure and LIME on M3 use the joint prompt, not the label-only prompt | label-only prompt, which is about 2.5 times faster | The two prompts gave different label sets on 5 of 17 sample posts at temperature 0, so the label-only prompt measures a different decision from the one S explains. | 2026-09-25 | A-vs-S overlap uses k = \|S_evid\|; k = \|G_evid\| only for A-vs-G on correct predictions | k = \|G_evid\| throughout, as first written | \|G_evid\| is 0 on every false positive, which would score A-vs-S overlap 0 on every incorrect prediction by construction and manufacture the H1 result |
| 2026-09-25 | Rationales are embedded with nomic-embed-text and no task prefix | "clustering: ", "classification: ", "search_document: " | nomic-embed-text was trained with task prefixes and Ollama adds none, so this is a real choice. Only 21 paraphrased texts, so re-check on the real data before any number is reported. The same probe put other-symptom rationales at 0.69 cosine with each other, which is why the null baseline matters next to ReDSM5's 0.78 |
| 2026-09-25 | Integrated Gradients written by hand over the word embeddings, not taken from Captum | captum.attr.LayerIntegratedGradients, as the plan said | Same reason as LIME: about twenty lines I can defend, with the path, baseline and step rule stated in the code. Captum 0.9.0 would have installed fine, so this is not a compatibility workaround. Cross-checked against Captum's LayerIntegratedGradients on the word-embedding layer of a small BERT with non-trivial weights: per-token attributions agreed to 7e-7 on attributions up to 1.9, with the same completeness gap at 20 and 300 steps |
| 2026-09-25 | The IG baseline is [PAD] at every position except [CLS] and [SEP]; [UNK] is not kept | all-[PAD] including the specials; all-[MASK] | Keeping [CLS] and [SEP] means only the words move along the path, so the specials get exactly zero attribution and cannot crowd out sentences. | 2026-09-25 | IG runs 50 midpoint steps in batches of 10 | 100 steps; batches of 25 | On full-size bert-base at 512 tokens on the Apple GPU: 6.3s per (post, symptom) at batch 10, about 47 minutes for the test set, with a completeness gap of 3e-3 on an untrained head. | 2026-09-25 | Negative affect is counted with the NRC Emotion Lexicon's fear, anger and sadness words | LIWC negemo (paid); NRC 'negative'; VADER; the Hu and Liu opinion lexicon | The literature this checks against (Rude et al. 2004) used LIWC negative emotion, which is anxiety + anger + sadness; these are the matching NRC emotions. NRC is licensed for research: downloaded by hand, read from data/, gitignored |
| 2026-09-25 | Markers are rates per word; present tense is the share of finite verbs (VBP, VBZ against VBD) | raw counts; present-tense verbs per word | Rates let a short attributed sentence be compared with a long unattributed one, which is what the marker-density check does. Tense only shows on finite verbs, so dividing by all words would mix tense with how verb-heavy the text is. nan where there is nothing to divide by, left for the caller to handle |
| 2026-09-25 | Absolutist words are the 19-word dictionary of Al-Mosaiwi and Johnstone (2018) | a longer list of my own | It was validated on mental-health forum text, which is close to Reddit. | 2026-09-25 | M1 is one L2 logistic regression per label (C = 1) on TF-IDF 1-2 grams with min_df 2, plus the four markers standardised on the training posts | sklearn OneVsRestClassifier; tuned C; an associative classifier | One regression per label is what one-vs-rest is, written so every head's weights are in reach and a label that never varies in training gets a constant instead of a crash. C is left at the default because there is no validation split to tune it on without changing the reproduction. Markers are standardised so their weights are on a scale comparable with each other; a missing marker becomes the training mean |
| 2026-09-25 | TF-IDF keeps one-letter tokens, so "I" is a feature | sklearn's default token pattern, which drops tokens under two characters | The default silently removes "I", the most studied word in depression language, and every bigram containing it ("i cant", "i feel"). Found by a failing test |
| 2026-09-25 | M1's sentence score is the sentence's exact share of the label's logit; markers, boundary-crossing bigrams and the bias are reported separately | sum of the weights of features present in the sentence, as the plan said | A linear logit is a sum of weight * value terms, so each n-gram's term can be split across the sentences it occurs in by occurrence count, and sentences + markers + crossing + bias equals the logit exactly. That is what faithful by construction means, and it is tested as an equality. Summing bare weights would ignore TF-IDF values and double-count repeated n-grams. Markers are post-level rates and cannot be split into sentences, so their contribution is reported rather than spread |
| 2026-09-25 | The harness reads one JSONL record per (model, post, symptom) in a fixed format, documented at the top of src/evaluate.py | evaluate.py calling the models itself | Every upstream runner (IG over M2, the M3 joint and erasure runs, M1) writes the same format, so the harness is built and tested now on synthetic records, and a rerun of the metrics never re-runs a model. | 2026-09-25 | The M3 runner writes a record for every symptom the model predicted or the psychologist marked, and none for a post where both say no symptoms | records for predicted symptoms only | False negatives carry no rationale and are excluded from the TP-vs-FP analysis, but writing them keeps the correctness counts and per-symptom support complete in the harness |

| 2026-09-25 | Kept NRC fear + anger + sadness after checking its coverage of depression vocabulary; the gaps are a stated limitation | add NRC 'negative'; hand-add words such as 'empty' | Checked on the downloaded lexicon: 'hopeless' and 'exhausted' are covered, but 'empty' carries no tag at all and 'tired' and 'numb' are tagged only 'negative', which is sentiment polarity.
